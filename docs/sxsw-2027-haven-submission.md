## Why HAVEN can win here (research-verified)

- **AI is saturated; this angle is empty.** Nearly one-third of all
  2026 proposals touched AI (SXSW's own stat), and press called the
  fatigue out ("too broad, too generic"). But searches across the
  2025/2026 programs found **zero sessions** on anti-trafficking
  technology, federated learning, differential privacy, or hotel AI.
- **The topic already belongs on SXSW stages.** Nadia Murad's 2025
  featured session proved trafficking draws SXSW audiences (hers was
  advocacy — no technology); 2026's "Inside the International Scam
  Syndicate" proved crime-fighting-tech sessions get picked.
- **The ITU brand has SXSW precedent.** ITU's AI for Good team
  presented "Quantum for Good" at SXSW 2025 — and has never fielded a
  concrete case study like this one.
- **Selection is 40% evaluators / 30% staff / 30% public vote**, with
  staff explicitly filling topic gaps and courting "less well-known
  voices." The 2026 programming chief's stated filter: "how does this
  impact humans and humanity?" — which is exactly this entry's frame.

## How Mia presents this without a technical background

The session is a story with numbers, not math. Three beats:

1. **The problem is human.** 75% of survivors report hotel contact.
   Traffickers rotate properties on purpose: they know hotels cannot
   compare notes. Tell one (anonymized, composite) story of a pattern
   spread across three hotels that no single front desk could see.
2. **The idea in one metaphor.** Every hotel keeps a locked diary.
   HAVEN never opens anyone's diary — each hotel's AI reads its own
   diary at home and shares only the *lesson it learned*, never a page,
   never a name. Add noise on top ("a little static, so no single
   guest's voice can ever be picked out of the chorus") — that's
   differential privacy in one sentence. Twelve simulated hotels
   learning together caught 82% of high-risk patterns; alone, they
   caught far less.
3. **What it takes to reach a real lobby.** Front-desk training, false
   positives (8% — what that means for a real guest), who acts on an
   alert (staff, never automation), and the ask: pilot partners.

Hamed's role: the "how it really works" 5 minutes and technical Q&A.
Every number in the talk is in the ITU case study and SSRN preprint —
Mia never has to defend the math; she points to it.
