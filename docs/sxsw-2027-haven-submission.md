## Why HAVEN can win here (research-verified)

- **AI is saturated; this angle is empty.** Nearly one-third of all
  2026 proposals touched AI (SXSW's own stat), and press called the
  fatigue out ("too broad, too generic"). But searches across the
  2025/2026 programs found **zero sessions** on anti-trafficking
  technology, federated learning, differential privacy, or hotel AI.
- **The topic already belongs on SXSW stages.** Nadia Murad's 2025
  featured session proved trafficking draws SXSW audiences (hers was
  advocacy — no technology); 2026's "Inside the International Scam
  Syndicate" proved crime-fighting-tech sessions get picked.
- **The ITU brand has SXSW precedent.** ITU's AI for Good team
  presented "Quantum for Good" at SXSW 2025 — and has never fielded a
  concrete case study like this one.
- **Selection is 40% evaluators / 30% staff / 30% public vote**, with
  staff explicitly filling topic gaps and courting "less well-known
  voices." The 2026 programming chief's stated filter: "how does this
  impact humans and humanity?" — which is exactly this entry's frame.

## FIELD 2 — Format and track

- **Format: Presentation** (1–2 speakers, "a single sharp
  perspective"). Panels are the most-pitched format; a founder-led
  presentation with one co-speaker reads differentiated.
- **Track: Tech & AI.** SXSW restructured 2027 into 11 tracks (the
  standalone AI and civic-engagement tracks are gone); Tech & AI's own
  description covers technology "and society," and no other 2027 track
  genuinely fits. It's the crowded lane — the anti-trafficking
  specificity is what separates the entry from the ~1/3-of-everything
  AI pile.
- **Scale check:** main SXSW accepts roughly 1 in 8–10 PanelPicker
  entries (450+ sessions from thousands of proposals) — similar odds
  to EDU, decided 40% expert evaluators / 30% staff / 30% public vote.

## FIELD 3 — Description (limit 600 characters; this is 596)

> Three of four U.S. sex-trafficking survivors report contact with
> hotels during their exploitation. Traffickers rotate across
> properties precisely because privacy law and duty of care stop hotels
> from comparing notes — no single property ever sees the pattern. This
> session shows how a multi-agent federated network resolves that
> paradox: per-property AI agents learn locally, share only protected
> model updates, and guest data never leaves the building. We walk
> through the ITU AI for Good–featured architecture, 12-property
> simulation results, and what it takes to move from math to hotel
> lobby.

(Paste as one paragraph. Honesty guardrails baked in: results are
called **simulation** results — never imply live hotel deployment —
and the ITU credential uses the exact phrase from the case page:
"Featured in AI for Good.")
