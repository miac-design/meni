## Organization summary (short)

Austin AI Hub teaches practical AI skills to the people the tech industry
has left behind: senior citizens with no technology background and
underserved communities in Austin, Texas. We run free workshops and monthly
AI office hours, and we build Meni — a daily micro-learning app designed
seniors-first, in co-design with the participants themselves.

## The problem

AI use among adults 50+ nearly doubled in a year (18% to 30%, AARP 2026),
and AI-powered scams now specifically target older adults — yet no product
teaches AI literacy in a form this population can actually use. Existing
AI-learning apps (Sololearn, Brilliant) target professionals. Existing
senior tech education (Senior Planet, GetSetUp, Cyber-Seniors) is
scheduled, synchronous, class-based — if you miss Tuesday at 10am, you
missed it. Nobody offers daily, self-paced, senior-first AI learning, and
nobody pairs it with a recurring human touchpoint.

## What we built

Meni is a free web app: one lesson a day, one to two minutes, one idea,
one tap. It is designed around the strictest constraints our community
has — aging vision, tremor, low literacy, low tech confidence, and
trauma-informed needs:

- Every lesson can be read aloud; no login, no account, no personal data
  ever collected — everything stays on the learner's phone
- Progress is a garden that only grows: no streaks, no scores, no shame
- Spaced-repetition "watering days" revisit earlier lessons so learning
  sticks; a plain-words "What I can do now" list builds confidence
- Scam-spotting lessons (gift-card rule, AI voice cloning, prize scams)
  use the app's only alarm styling — everything else is warm
- Every screen has "Save this question for Meni": confusion becomes
  conversation material at our monthly office hours instead of a reason
  to quit. The app keeps the flame alive BETWEEN human touchpoints; it
  never replaces them.

Status: working product, 32 lessons across a seniors track and a
work-and-rights track, piloting with office-hours participants in July
2026. Co-designed with participants: name, colors, and mascot are being
preference-tested with the actual community before decisions are final.

## Why this fits the Fund's AI-literacy focus

This is AI literacy for the population at highest risk of AI-powered harm
and lowest access to AI's benefits — delivered in the only format that
meets them where they are: two minutes a day on the phone they already
own, plus a monthly human who knows their name.
