## Description (~150 words)

AI use among adults 50+ nearly doubled in a year — and AI-powered scams
aimed at them grew faster. Yet every AI-learning app targets
professionals, and every senior tech program is a scheduled class. What
does learning design look like when your users have aging eyes, tremor,
possible low literacy, possible trauma — and every mainstream engagement
mechanic (streaks, scores, loss, guilt) is actively harmful?

This session shares what we learned building Meni, a daily two-minute AI
literacy app co-designed with seniors and underserved communities in
Austin: why our progress metaphor is a garden that can never wilt, why
red appears only in scam lessons, why the mascot is never allowed to be
disappointed, and why the app's real retention engine is a monthly human,
not a notification. Come for the design heresy; leave with a playbook for
building for the people tech forgot.

## Three takeaways

1. A concrete accessibility playbook for designing daily-learning
   products for older adults: vision, motor, literacy, and
   trauma-informed rules that contradict standard engagement practice.
2. Why pairing micro-learning with a recurring human checkpoint
   outperforms both classes and apps alone — and how to structure it.
3. How to co-design with a community instead of for it: preference
   testing, participant advisors, and dignity as a design requirement.
